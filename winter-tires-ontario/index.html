Ontario Winter Tire Guide · Updated July 2026

When Should You Put On Winter Tires in Ontario?

Short answer: when daytime highs consistently sit below 7°C — in Halton, that's usually late October to mid-November. Here's the why, the insurance discount most drivers forget to claim, and how to skip the November scramble entirely.

The 7°C rule (it's about cold, not snow)

Winter tires aren't "snow tires" — they're cold tires. Below about 7°C, the rubber compound in all-season tires begins to harden, and hard rubber grips less. That means longer stopping distances on cold, dry pavement — weeks before the first flake falls on the QEW.

Winter compounds stay soft and flexible in the cold, and their deeper, sipe-heavy tread bites into snow and slush. The result is measurably shorter braking and better cornering in every cold condition Halton throws at you — including the freeze-thaw mornings on the escarpment side of Burlington and Halton Hills, where cold arrives earlier than by the lake.

Are winter tires required by law in Ontario?

No. Unlike Quebec, Ontario has no winter tire mandate. But there's a financial nudge: since January 2016, Ontario insurers have been required to offer a discount — commonly around 2–5% of your premium — when you install four matching winter tires. Call your broker with your installation date and keep the receipt. A mobile installation receipt from your driveway counts exactly like a shop's.

The smart booking window (and the November trap)

Every year, the first forecast snowfall triggers the same stampede: every tire bay in the GTA books out for two to three weeks, right when you need the tires most. The smart window is mid-to-late October — cold enough that the winters work, early enough that you choose the appointment instead of the weather choosing it for you.

Quick answers

Is there a specific date to switch?

No fixed date — follow the 7°C rule. For insurance discounts, some policies specify an installation window (often by Nov 1 or Dec 1); check your policy's wording.

Can I run winters year-round?

You can, but you'll wear them out fast and grip suffers in warm weather — soft winter compounds squirm and overheat above ~7°C. Swap back in April.

Do EVs need different winter tires?

EVs are heavier and torquier, so XL-load-rated winters (ideally OE acoustic versions) are the right call. We source and install them — EV tire service.

Are all-weather tires (3PMSF) good enough?

Three-peak-mountain-snowflake all-weathers are a legitimate one-set compromise for light-duty city driving, and they usually qualify for the insurance discount. For escarpment commutes and highway kilometres, dedicated winters still stop shorter.
